Branch B: mechanism investigation and static-weight repair

Executive conclusion

Thirty distinct static-weight candidates were fully evaluated on the same development set. The harness-selected candidate is round 14, c-37a38c134e5e044e609e621db2a8fab7e666bfe04dfdaecfe7340464a1333851, a geometry-matched random-retention control on decoder layer 34 MLP output column 1057. The official evaluation improves accuracy from 320/464 (68.97%) to 322/464 (69.40%), with two corrections, no correct-to-incorrect regressions, and one additional wrong-to-wrong answer change. The repair score rises from 55/116 (0.474137931) to 56/116 (0.482758621). The primary T0 joint score remains 27/58 (0.465517241).

These are small, selected, seen-development gains—not evidence of a general spatial-reasoning repair. Both corrected zebra prompts repeated successfully in the final audit, but one has a measured 0-versus-1 logit tie. The best candidate also has two evaluation/repeat disagreements outside those corrections. The most defensible mechanistic conclusion is that the investigated components affect context-dependent competition among numerical answers. A shared spatial-success direction, a uniquely necessary zero-aligned component, and a serial spatial circuit were not established.

Round 30 has the highest raw development accuracy, 325/464, but ties round 14 on the repair score and was not selected by the harness. Automatic selection was preserved; no alternative tie-breaking rule or post-diagnostic rescoring was introduced.

Scope and measurements

The public data are branch B, development split D: 58 image groups, each with eight prompts spanning total/subset counting, T0/T1 wording, and B0/B1 prompting. B1 adds a generic instruction to identify the reference and apply the spatial conditions. The 464 prompts are not 464 independent observations. The repair metric uses 116 B0 total/subset pairs, still clustered within 58 image groups.

The model is Qwen3-VL with 36 decoder layers, hidden width 2560, MLP width 9728, and 32 attention output blocks of width 128. Investigations used complete generated answers, selected digit logits, and captured activations. Captures at the last prompt token measure a text-position state, not a visual token or an object-specific spatial variable. Static weight interventions apply at every position, unlike temporary last-token probes.

The actual W0 evaluation, not the bundle's previous predictions, is the reference. There were discrepancies between those sources and also between some evaluations and fresh repeats. Input-token and rendered-chat hashes were checked. Array hashes, token positions, and upstream invariance checks support implementation validity, but do not remove numerical execution sensitivity.

All 30 candidates had zero recorded format errors and zero truncated outputs in their full evaluations. No candidate lost more than one correct answer in any 58-prompt cell. No held-out validation, branch-C evaluation, or generalization test was performed.

Localization and competing explanations

Initial same-image failure/success paraphrase contrasts did not support one shared spatial-success direction: the contrasts mixed different numerical answer changes, and early effects were strongly associated with prompt form. Removing two prompt-form axes did not eliminate answer confounding. The resulting zero-success directions were therefore treated as observational hypotheses, not identified spatial representations.

Component analysis nominated MLP output columns L23:3537, L32:619, L34:1057, and attention output block L24:head13. L34 channel 1057 was strongly active on answer-one examples, including correct-one controls, while a wrong-three/zero-gold strawberry counterexample had little activity. Answer-free clarifications also failed to repair most cases and damaged a correct dog-count response. These observations weaken a simple spatial-condition-omission account.

Temporary removal of the L34 channel's last-token input changed the zebra 1-versus-0 margin from +0.5 to -0.25, but did not flip the harder tennis-ball or laptop failures. The correct-one control also changed in margin without changing answer. Static full-column removal reproduced the two zebra corrections. This supports a local causal influence on numerical outputs; it does not identify what spatial information, if any, the channel represents.

Relevant evidence: actions 0012–0019, 0022–0025, 0027–0029, and the fresh full-ablation reference in actions 0153/0155.

Controls that constrain the mechanism claim
L34 direction decomposition and randomized controls

Removing only the zero-aligned projection of column 1057 did not produce the two zebra corrections. Retaining that projection while removing its orthogonal component did. Thus, removing the proposed zero-aligned component was not necessary for those observed repairs. The latter intervention also increased the correct tennis-total one-versus-zero margin, contradicting uniform suppression of answer one.

Transferring the edit to three other L34 channels gave no corresponding repairs, but these controls were weak: global activity matching did not match their activity on the critical answer-one cases. The conditional matching search found no eligible channels; thresholds were not relaxed. Same-channel randomized-delta controls matched edit magnitude but not the new column norm, so they also did not isolate direction cleanly.

Three geometry-matched random-retention controls matched the new-column norm and its inner product with the original column before BF16 rounding. All three reproduced both zebra corrections in their official evaluations. Round 14 is one of these controls. This favors generic attenuation as a plausible explanation over a special role for retaining the measured zero direction. It does not prove that every similarly sized attenuation would behave identically.

Evidence: rounds 4–14, actions 0048–0050, the saved geometry-control plan, and round-14 provenance. The full ledger retains wrong-to-wrong changes and the unstable umbrella corrections rather than counting all changed answers as mechanistic successes.

L23/L32 dose tests

L32 column-619 attenuation and L23 column-3537 attenuation also changed the zebra margins, but effects were contextual. The L32 half-ablation's evaluated zebra correction did not repeat. Full L23 ablation repaired additional zero-count prompts but introduced three total-count regressions. Enhancement controls repaired other cases, not the target zebra failures. Correct-one controls and the wrong-three counterexample rule out a universal one-suppression or spatial-repair interpretation.

Evidence: rounds 15–20, actions 0087/0089, 0094, 0098, 0104, 0108, and 0113.

L24 head experiments

Head 13 attenuation repaired the two zebra prompts and some other development failures, but full ablation also damaged a people-total answer. A preregistered head-24 control was matched approximately on block/edit norm and diagnostic write magnitude; it did not repair the two zebra targets, although it repaired other cases. This is evidence for a relative difference between these two tested head interventions, not proof that head 13 uniquely implements spatial filtering. The control did not match every semantic or activation property, and head edits were much larger than single-column MLP edits.

The complementary head-13 decomposition again matters: retaining the zero-aligned projection while removing the orthogonal component preserved both zebra corrections. The predicted direct change along that direction was nearly zero, whereas whole-layer changes included subsequent MLP computation. Conversely, the projection-removal candidate had evaluation/repeat disagreements on both zebra cases. Whole-layer residual changes must not be equated with isolated head writes.

Evidence: rounds 21–27, actions 0115/0117, 0118, 0123, 0129, 0133, 0137, 0141, 0145/0147, and 0151; L24_head_experiments_21_27.json.

Fixed pair tests

The three final pairs were specified before their outcomes, independently reconstructed from W0, and each zeroed exactly two MLP output columns (5120 changed scalars). They were not added cumulatively to head edits.

  • L23+L32, round 28: 323/464, six corrections and three regressions. Two corrected zebra prompt variants were additional to the union of the single-edit corrections.
  • L23+L34, round 29: 322/464, five corrections and the same three total-count regressions. One additional zebra prompt variant was corrected.
  • L32+L34, round 30: 325/464, five corrections and no regressions. Tennis-ball B1 and laptop B1 corrections were additional to the single-edit union. The umbrella correction is not secure causal evidence because W0 itself repeats as the correct answer.

All three fixed 13-prompt diagnostic panels matched their respective evaluated complete answers, with no truncation, and passed the planned ordering checks. This does not cover every changed prompt outside those panels.

Margin interaction was defined as m_pair - m_first - m_second + m_W0. Results included small nonadditivities. For example, in round 29, the incremental L34 one-versus-zero effect was smaller after L23 ablation on the two zebra cases; on the correct tennis-total control, the standalone L34 effect was +0.5 but the conditional effect was -0.25. BF16 resolution and near ties matter. Zero interactions in some pre-output activations follow from causal ordering of output-column edits; they do not prove independence.

Overlapping repairs, changed downstream activity, and pair nonadditivity do not establish serial mediation. No path-specific restoration, edge intervention, or causal mediation experiment isolated such a route. Evidence: actions 0157/0158/0160, 0164/0165/0166, 0170/0171/0172, and the three pair-diagnostic audit files.

Identical prompt hashes and repeated capture hashes do not establish why evaluation and diagnostic execution differed. No unsupported backend explanation is asserted.

Limits and next discriminating tests
  1. All localization, candidate design, evaluation, and selection used development data. Thirty adaptive candidates and repeated prompt variants preclude an unqualified generalization or statistical-significance claim.
  2. The selected gain is two prompts on two seen zebra images; the B0 repair metric gains only one paired success. Primary T0 joint performance does not improve.
  3. Several effects occur at BF16 ties or very small margins. The fresh diagnostic audit was limited to 15 selected-candidate prompts, not a fresh full-development evaluation.
  4. The components are answer-confounded and affect correct counting as well as failures. Negative controls were imperfect and some predeclared predictions failed.
  5. Static output edits do not isolate spatial information flow. No unique spatial circuit, path mediation, or broad ability restoration is claimed.

The appropriate next tests would use untouched image groups with balanced answer counts, matched relation/reference counterfactuals, execution-matched repeat audits, and path-specific restoration of candidate downstream signals. These are proposals, not completed experiments, and no additional candidate was created after round 30.

主要结论

最有根据的综合收益不是“两份最佳补丁互补很多”，而是发现粗调与细调高度重合的行为覆盖，并用第二轮控制实验削弱“已找到特定空间失败方向”的解释。存在一个值得保留的新增覆盖案例，但不足以支持大量协同收益；跨版本权重组合从未执行。

4. 两次结果合起来更支持什么解释

已测行为：v8最终补丁的13修复与9损伤全部伴随数字下降；但第47轮有3条向上修复，所以不是所有粗调都只压低数字。v9选中补丁的两条修复也是1→0。不同参数位置影响了高度重叠的少量错误。

v9控制与重复记录进一步显示：L34:1057的三个几何匹配随机保留方向都复现两条斑马修复；保留原提议的零相关投影也能修复，删除该投影并非必要。部分正确计数也受影响，若干例子处于logit近零/相等边界，官方批量评价与诊断重复出现差异。研究者报告把可支持结论限制为“这些组件影响依赖上下文的数字答案竞争”，不声称找到了通用空间成功方向、唯一通道或串行空间回路。

综合推断：两版可能反复碰到了同一小群容易翻转的回答，而未普遍修复空间关系计算。这是由覆盖重合与v9控制共同支持的工作解释，仍不能确定v8是否通过v9定位的通道起作用；两个不同干预产生同样回答，不证明内部路径相同。上游norm校准影响后续通道活动，是可检验但本次未测的联系，不能写成已知链条。

另一方面，v9内部已有双列组合产生单列修复并集之外的修复，提示非线性组合并非没有可能。因此，“两个最佳的修复集合重叠”不能证明实际叠加必然无效。可说的是目前缺乏期待大幅收益的直接证据，跨版本组合的符号和大小未知。

5. 本次能交付的收获
  • 一份跨80候选的逐题覆盖账，识别共同105条未修复错误，防止把更多同类搜索当作覆盖已扩大。
  • 一个粗调始终不变、细调能翻转的明确输入，连同B1限定和重复检查缺口。
  • 两组非赢家的有限互补与选择规则取舍，说明最终分数不足以总结整个搜索。
  • 更受约束的机制解释：局部数值决策影响有证据，特定空间失败机制未建立。

本次不新增实验，也不把方向建议升级为执行计划。若以后研究跨版本组合，需要另行说明它要验证哪条联系；不能以本次答案并集代替组合测量。
